BLOG MATHEMATICS AUDIT — 27 OCTOBER 2026
========================================

Scope and standard
------------------
Reviewed every article source and all folded proofs in the current catalogue:
35 published articles and 31 forthcoming articles. Four are announcements or
commentary without mathematical theorems; the other 62 contain mathematical
exposition. Old-preview redirects were resolved to their actual published source.
Untracked ideas and authoring notes are not published/forthcoming articles.

This is an independent mathematical and numerical review, not formal verification
or a guarantee of error-free mathematics. Proofs were checked for assumptions,
quantifiers, boundary cases, constants, matrix order, and consistency with the
experiments. Selected specialized claims were compared with primary references.
No additional established defect was found beyond the corrections below.

Article changes
---------------
1. Muon as a spectral Wasserstein flow:
   - Put gamma on the PSD cone, so the trace example is correctly a gauge
     (the restriction of the nuclear norm), not a norm on all symmetric matrices.
   - Specify the partial polar factor using strictly positive singular values,
     including the zero-gradient convention.
   - State a selected steepest velocity rather than incorrectly suggesting
     uniqueness at rank-deficient gradients.
   - Give the normalized particle variational objective and its dual-norm argument.

2. Spectral Wasserstein distances:
   - Restrict possible triangle-inequality failure for 0 < p < 1/2 to d >= 2.
     In dimension one every positive p reduces to ordinary W2.
   - Credit Appendix E.4 of the paper's October 2026 v3 for the extension,
     replacing the outdated statement that the paper covers only p >= 1.

3. Straighter flows, smaller Euler errors:
   - Explicitly state b >= 0 for the experiment whose acceleration bound is b*pi^2.

4. From Perron-Frobenius to Sinkhorn:
   - Explicitly require epsilon > 0 for the entropy-minimization/scaling problem.

5. Fast gradient flows without convexity:
   - Add a folded, explicit proof that no positive global PL constant exists for
     b > 0: cancel the first gradient coordinate and obtain the ratio 2/(1+c^2).
   - Explain why the sublevel theorem remains valid and why b = 0 is different.
   - This strengthens the justification of an already correct statement.

6. Optimal conditioning of low-rank adaptation:
   - Explicitly require nonzero rank, 1 <= r < min(n,m), so the extreme positive
     singular values and condition number are defined.

Publication dates, titles, approvals, announcement text, numerical assets and
forthcoming/published status have not been changed. Changed published articles
have last_modified_at set to 2026-10-27.

Outstanding limitations and unrelated findings
----------------------------------------------
- The original SciPy-dependent CLT/ICNN optimization regeneration could not be
  run because SciPy is not installed. No packages were installed. The independent
  standard-library CLT test covers every saved uniform value; ICNN model hashes,
  stored LP witness evaluations and geometric checks passed, but the LP optimizer
  was not rerun.
- The hump-algebra article invokes an external Gaussian-atomic/Besov theorem.
  Its scaling, displayed derivations and dictionary were checked; the full Meyer
  chapter/Brandolese PDF was unavailable for a fresh proof-level source check.
  This is a source-verification limitation, not an established counterexample.
- Standard invoked results (Brenier, Strassen, superposition, nonsmooth Jacobian
  facts, etc.) remain dependencies; their complete proofs were not rederived.
- Browser-only subscription test was not run: a broad test discovery reached it
  and stopped at its missing Playwright dependency. Actual browser checks used
  the app browser. No claim is made of exhaustive browser/UI testing of all posts.
- Existing editorial-queue tests have THREE failures, unrelated to mathematical
  edits: four completed articles remain in the active queue rather than the
  archive, and ready entries are not all ordered first. The four articles are
  max/softmax, Lagrangian interpolation, DC/Toland, and Langevin.
  These overlap user-edited queue state; neither queue file was changed or staged.
- Existing .DS_Store changes and untracked blog/ideas files were preserved.
