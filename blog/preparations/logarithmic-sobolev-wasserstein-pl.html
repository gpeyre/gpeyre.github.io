Logarithmic Sobolev is a Wasserstein P–L inequality

An entropy inequality controls both the objective and the distance travelled by a distribution.

Gabriel Peyré

A small gradient is reassuring only if it means that the objective is nearly minimized. The Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition supplies precisely this guarantee [6]. It can yield exponential convergence even without convexity, as in Fast gradient flows without convexity.

Now replace a point by a probability distribution, Euclidean distance by quadratic Wasserstein distance, and the objective by relative entropy. The same condition becomes the logarithmic Sobolev inequality. This is more than a dictionary: one short argument shows that the entire distribution converges, not just its entropy. It also explains the implication from logarithmic Sobolev to Talagrand’s transport inequality [1].

Entropy has a gradient: Fisher information

Let \(\pi(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}\) be a smooth positive probability density on \(\mathbb R^d\). We use natural logarithms and write

\[ \mathcal F(\rho)=\operatorname{KL}(\rho\mid\pi) =\int\rho\log\frac\rho\pi\,dx,\qquad \mathcal I(\rho\mid\pi)=\int\left|\nabla\log\frac\rho\pi\right|^2\rho\,dx. \]

For the calculations, assume finite second moments and enough smoothness and decay to integrate by parts. If a density moves with velocity \(v\), the continuity equation is \(\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0\). Differentiating gives

\[ \frac d{dt}\mathcal F(\rho_t) =\int\nabla\log(\rho_t/\pi)\cdot v_t\,\rho_t\,dx. \]

The tangent norm is \(\|v\|_\rho^2=\int|v|^2\rho\). Thus the Wasserstein gradient is represented by \(\nabla\log(\rho/\pi)\), and its squared norm is Fisher information. Its negative gradient flow is

\[ v_t=-\nabla\log(\rho_t/\pi),\qquad \partial_t\rho_t=\Delta\rho_t+\nabla\cdot(\rho_t\nabla V). \]

This is the Fokker–Planck equation. Along a regular solution,

\[ -\frac d{dt}\mathcal F(\rho_t) =\mathcal I(\rho_t\mid\pi) =|\dot\rho_t|_{W_2}^2. \]

The equality with metric speed uses the gradient velocity, not an arbitrary velocity solving the same continuity equation. Wasserstein gradient-flow theory makes this identity precise beyond smooth densities [2].

The P–L condition in disguise

Proposition 1 (the dictionary). Logarithmic Sobolev with constant \(\lambda>0\) is the inequality

\[ \mathcal I(\rho\mid\pi)\ge 2\lambda\operatorname{KL}(\rho\mid\pi). \]

Equivalently,

\[ \tfrac12\|\operatorname{grad}_{W_2}\mathcal F(\rho)\|_\rho^2 \ge\lambda(\mathcal F(\rho)-\min\mathcal F). \]

This is exactly Wasserstein P–L. Consequently,

\[ \mathcal F(\rho_t)\le e^{-2\lambda t}\mathcal F(\rho_0). \]
Proof: substitute the gradient norm

The minimum of relative entropy is zero, attained only at \(\pi\). Substitute the gradient–Fisher identity above. Energy dissipation then gives \(\mathcal F'\le-2\lambda\mathcal F\), and Grönwall gives the decay estimate. \(\square\)

The constant convention matters: for the standard Gaussian, \(\lambda=1\). In the classical function formulation, putting \(\rho=h^2\pi/\int h^2d\pi\) turns this into \(\operatorname{Ent}_\pi(h^2)\le(2/\lambda)\int|\nabla h|^2d\pi\). This is the normalization of Gross’s Gaussian inequality [3].

Why the measures themselves converge

Exponential decay of an objective does not, by itself, imply convergence of its argument. A valley can contain many minimizers; a curve might keep travelling along it. Here the dissipation identity gives the missing control: the curve has finite remaining length.

Theorem 1 (finite length under Wasserstein P–L). Let \(\mathcal G:\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\to(-\infty,+\infty]\) be lower semicontinuous, with finite infimum \(\mathcal G_\star\). Suppose a global locally absolutely continuous flow, of finite initial energy, has locally absolutely continuous energy \(E\) and satisfies for almost every time

\[ E(t)=\mathcal G(\rho_t)-\mathcal G_\star,\qquad -E'(t)=|\dot\rho_t|_{W_2}^2=|\partial\mathcal G|^2(\rho_t), \qquad |\partial\mathcal G|^2(\rho_t)\ge2\lambda E(t). \]

Here \(|\partial\mathcal G|\) is the descending metric slope. Then the flow converges in \(W_2\) to a minimizer \(\rho_\infty\), and

\[ W_2(\rho_t,\rho_\infty) \le\sqrt{\frac{2E(t)}\lambda} \le\sqrt{\frac{2E(0)}\lambda}\,e^{-\lambda t}. \]
Proof: differentiate the square root of the energy

Write \(s(t)=|\dot\rho_t|_{W_2}\). As long as \(E(t)>0\), dissipation and P–L give

\[ -\frac d{dt}\sqrt{E(t)} =\frac{s(t)^2}{2\sqrt{E(t)}} \ge\sqrt{\lambda/2}\,s(t). \]

Integrate from \(a\) to \(b\), and use that distance is bounded by the length of a curve:

\[ W_2(\rho_a,\rho_b) \le\int_a^b s(t)\,dt \le\sqrt{2/\lambda}\bigl(\sqrt{E(a)}-\sqrt{E(b)}\bigr). \]

Also \(E'\le-2\lambda E\), so \(E(t)\to0\). The last bound makes the curve Cauchy as time tends to infinity. Completeness of \(\mathcal P_2\) gives a limit, and lower semicontinuity gives \(\mathcal G(\rho_\infty)\le\liminf\mathcal G(\rho_t)=\mathcal G_\star\). Let \(b\to\infty\) in the length bound. If energy reaches zero at a finite time, the dissipation identity makes the curve constant thereafter, with the same conclusion. \(\square\)

This proves convergence to the limit selected by the initial condition. It does not assert that two different flows contract toward one another. Neither uniqueness of the minimizer nor displacement convexity is required in Theorem 1.

For entropy, the minimizer is uniquely \(\pi\). Applying the theorem at time zero gives

\[ W_2(\rho,\pi)^2\le\frac2\lambda\operatorname{KL}(\rho\mid\pi). \]

This is Talagrand’s quadratic transportation inequality, with the same constant. The calculation proves it for initial data admitting the stated dissipative flow; standard approximation extends it to all finite-entropy data under the usual hypotheses. The full logarithmic-Sobolev-to-transport implication is due to Otto and Villani [1].

A Gaussian example attaining both rates

Take \(\pi=\mathcal N(0,I)\) and \(\rho_0=\mathcal N(m_0,I)\). The exact flow is \(\rho_t=\mathcal N(e^{-t}m_0,I)\). Hence

\[ \mathcal F(\rho_t)=\tfrac12e^{-2t}|m_0|^2,\qquad W_2(\rho_t,\pi)=e^{-t}|m_0|. \]

The objective decays at rate two, the distance at rate one. Both constants in Theorem 1 are sharp.

Why the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev constant is one

Here is a short semigroup proof for smooth positive densities \(f=d\rho/d\pi\) bounded above and away from zero, with sufficient derivative integrability. Let

\[ P_tf(x)=\mathbb E\,f(e^{-t}x+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}Z),\qquad Z\sim\pi. \]

Gaussian invariance gives \(\int P_tg\,d\pi=\int g\,d\pi\). Differentiation gives \(\nabla P_tf=e^{-t}P_t\nabla f\). Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz therefore yields

\[ \frac{|\nabla P_tf|^2}{P_tf} \le e^{-2t}P_t\!\left(\frac{|\nabla f|^2}{f}\right). \]

After integration, \(\mathcal I(P_tf\,\pi\mid\pi)\le e^{-2t}\mathcal I(f\pi\mid\pi)\). Entropy dissipation and \(P_tf\to1\) give

\[ \operatorname{KL}(f\pi\mid\pi) =\int_0^\infty\mathcal I(P_tf\,\pi\mid\pi)\,dt \le\tfrac12\mathcal I(f\pi\mid\pi). \]

Boundedness justifies the entropy limit by dominated convergence. Truncation, smoothing, and lower semicontinuity extend the inequality to its natural finite-information domain. \(\square\)

More than entropy, more than convexity

Uniformly convex confinement. If \(\nabla^2V\succeq\lambda I\), the Bakry–Émery criterion gives logarithmic Sobolev with constant \(\lambda\) [4]. This is a sufficient condition, not a characterization: a bounded perturbation of a uniformly convex potential can preserve a logarithmic Sobolev inequality even when pointwise convexity is lost.

A genuinely nonconvex lifted loss. If \(|\nabla h|^2\ge2\lambda(h-h_\star)\), integration proves Wasserstein P–L for \(\mathcal G(\rho)=\int h\,d\rho\) whenever its slope has the expected gradient representation. For instance,

\[ h(x,y)=\tfrac12(y-x^2)^2,\qquad |\nabla h|^2=(1+4x^2)(y-x^2)^2\ge2h. \]

Its minimizers are distributions supported on a parabola. A particle stays in its initial sublevel, which bounds \(|y-x^2|\); the equations \(x'=2x(y-x^2)\), \(y'=-(y-x^2)\) then rule out finite-time escape. Theorem 1 applies to distributional flows for which its finite-moment and full-dissipation assumptions hold. Different initializations can select different minimizing measures.

Confined internal energy. For \(m>1\), the functional

\[ \mathcal G(\rho)=\frac1{m-1}\int\rho^m\,dx+ \frac\lambda2\int|x|^2\rho\,dx \]

is \(\lambda\)-displacement convex and satisfies Wasserstein P–L with constant \(\lambda\). Indeed, its geodesic subgradient inequality bounds the energy gap by \(|\partial\mathcal G|r-\lambda r^2/2\le|\partial\mathcal G|^2/(2\lambda)\), where \(r\) is the distance to a minimizer. Convexity supplies one route to P–L; the finite-length argument needs only P–L itself. See Optimal Transport for Machine Learners [5].
