Mixture and numerical method

The weights are (0.2, 0.5, 0.3), the component means are 1.35θ × (−1.1, −0.1, 0.9), and all three component variances are 1 − 0.49(1.35θ)². This variance stays positive, including at the endpoints. At θ = 0 the three components coincide with N(0, 1); negative θ reflects the positive-θ mixture.

All expectations are evaluated from exact Gaussian moments or characteristic functions, not random samples. Values are rounded for display. The column E[f(X)] gives the mean of the test itself; it need not vanish for a Gaussian. The last numerical column instead gives the Stein residual E[f′(X)] − E[X f(X)]. The plots show x between −4 and 4, but expectations use the whole real line. The density axes and residual-bar scale stay fixed as θ changes; the function plot has a separate vertical scale for each test.

These six tests are only a finite diagnostic, not a characterization of all distributions. Matching their expectations does not generally imply equality of measures. In this particular family, the cubic test detects every nonzero θ.
